Lesson 1 – Netiquette

What is Netiquette? Definition ▼

Netiquette (Network Etiquette) – a set of rules for behaving properly online. It is concerned with the "proper" way to communicate in an online environment.

It comes from Shea's Core Rules of Netiquette and applies to email, instant messaging, video calls, discussion boards, and social media.

Core Rules of Netiquette Key Concept ▼
#RuleMeaning
1Remember the HumanPractice the Golden Rule — treat others as you want to be treated.
2Same standards as real lifeDon't lower your ethical behavior just because you're online.
3Know where you areNetiquette varies by platform — what's OK in text may not be OK in email.
4Respect time & bandwidthKeep messages concise and meaningful; avoid unnecessary attachments.
5Make yourself look goodCheck spelling/grammar; be pleasant and polite.
6Share expert knowledgeShare what you know; post useful resources and references.
7Control flame warsDon't fuel angry online exchanges — guide the discussion back to a productive tone.
8Respect others' privacyHandle private information carefully; don't share without consent.
9Don't abuse your powerHaving more knowledge/power doesn't give you the right to take advantage of others.
10Be forgiving of mistakesNot everyone knows the rules — respond to errors privately and kindly.
Basic Netiquette Tips Practical ▼
  • Avoid slang, acronyms, and text talk – Use proper spelling and grammar (e.g., "your" not "ur").
  • Avoid SCREAMING – Typing in all caps is rude online. Use bold or italics for emphasis instead.
  • Proofread before sending – Check for spelling errors, missing content, and inaccurate information.
  • Exercise good judgment – Private messages should not be forwarded without consent. Don't share sensitive personal info online.
  • Respect diversity in viewpoints – Acknowledge others' perspectives; identify whose opinion you're sharing (e.g., "In my opinion…").
Ten Commandments of Computer Ethics Key Concept ▼
  1. Thou shalt not use a computer to harm other people.
  2. Thou shalt not interfere with other people's computer work.
  3. Thou shalt not snoop around in other people's computer files.
  4. Thou shalt not use a computer to steal.
  5. Thou shalt not use a computer to bear false witness.
  6. Thou shalt not copy or use proprietary software for which you have not paid (without permission).
  7. Thou shalt not use other people's computer resources without authorization or proper compensation.
  8. Thou shalt not appropriate other people's intellectual output.
  9. Thou shalt think about the social consequences of the program you are writing or the system you are designing.
  10. Thou shalt always use a computer in ways that ensure consideration and respect for other humans.

Lesson 2 – Cybercrimes

What is Cybercrime? Definition ▼

Cybercrime – a crime in which a computer is the object of the crime OR used as a tool to commit an offense (e.g., hacking, phishing, spamming, child pornography).

Cyber – refers to the characteristics of the culture of computers, information, technology, and virtual reality.

Virtual Self – the persona you create about yourself virtually.

Philippine Laws on Cybercrime Philippine Law ▼
  • RA 10175 – Cybercrime Prevention Act of 2012. Addresses legal issues concerning online interactions and the internet. Approved: September 12, 2012.
  • RA 10173 – Data Privacy Act of 2012. Protects individual personal information.
  • RA 10627 – Anti-Bullying Act of 2013. Covers cyberbullying.
  • RA 9775 – Anti-Child Pornography Act of 2009.

Lesson 3 – Internet Threats

Hacking Threat ▼

Hacking – unauthorized access to a computer system. Cyber criminals gain access by finding security weaknesses or installing a Trojan horse (back door).

Types: Illegal Access, Illegal Interception, Data Interference, System Interference, Misuse of Devices, Malware Infection.

Malware Threat ▼

Malware – malicious software that infects your computer. Includes viruses, worms, Trojan horses, spyware, and adware.

What it can do: intimidate via scareware, reformat hard drive, alter/delete files, steal sensitive info, send emails on your behalf, take control of your computer.

Pharming vs. Phishing Types ▼
TermDescriptionHow it Works
PharmingRedirects you to a fake website even if URL is entered correctlySpoofs a real site; tricks you into entering personal info
PhishingFake emails, texts, and websites disguised as authentic companiesTricks you into giving usernames, passwords, and credit card numbers. Also called "spoofing".
Ransomware Threat ▼

Ransomware – malware that restricts access to your computer/files and demands payment to remove the restriction. Spread via phishing emails or pop-up ads.

  • Lockscreen ransomware – displays an image blocking access to your computer.
  • Encryption ransomware – encrypts files on your hard drive (and sometimes USB/cloud drives).

What to do: Do NOT pay the ransom. Contact a reputable technician. Regularly back up your data to an external drive.

Spam Threat ▼

Spam – mass distribution of unsolicited messages, advertising, or pornography to addresses found online (social media, company websites, blogs).

What it can do: flood you with junk mail, trick you into clicking malicious links, serve as a vehicle for malware and fraud.

Spyware & Adware Threat ▼

Spyware/Adware – software that secretly collects personal information without your knowledge. Often disguised as a "free" download. Difficult to remove and can install viruses.

What it can do: send your passwords and browsing habits to third parties, change how your computer runs, redirect you to unwanted sites, flood you with pop-up ads.

Trojan Horses Threat ▼

Trojan Horse – a malicious program disguised as or embedded within legitimate software. It installs and runs automatically once downloaded.

What it can do: delete files, hack other computers using yours, watch you via webcam, log keystrokes (like credit card numbers), and record usernames/passwords.

Wi-Fi Eavesdropping Threat ▼

Wi-Fi Eavesdropping – virtual "listening in" on information shared over an unsecure (unencrypted) Wi-Fi network.

What it can do: potentially access your computer and steal logins, passwords, and other personal information.

Signs Your Computer Is Infected Checklist ▼
  • Takes longer than usual to start up, restarts on its own, or doesn't start at all.
  • Programs take a long time to launch.
  • Files and data have disappeared.
  • System and programs crash constantly.
  • Browser homepage has changed without your permission.
  • Web pages are slow to load or screen looks distorted.
  • Programs are running without your control.

What to do: Update and run your security software. If unsure, seek professional technical help.
